# Peer-Mentoring Playbook

## About

###### Objective

This project captures experience from participants in a self-organized
peer-mentoring groups and offers insights and suggestions on how run such a
group successfully. 


###### Rationale

Peer-Groups have the potential to provide entrypoints to the field. They also
have the potential to form a network that integrates newcomers into the
community.

Informal observations yield the impression that many groups are short-lived and
dissolve completely when their members leave. Also, many groups face similar
challenges and develop individual solutions.

This guide captures lessons-learned from peer-groups and offers advice and
boilerplate solutions to common problems. It is intended to be a living and
evolving collection of hopefully useful material.


## Definitions

- **Peer**: Someone who wants to learn the trade of the skill and is relatively
  new to the field -- on a _Beginner_, _Novice_, or _Proficient_ level -- and
  seeks support from others while to learning and building their skill-set.

- **Mentee**: A less experienced _Peer_ seeking to learn from more experienced
  _Peers_.

- **Mentor**: A _Peer_ with enough experience to assist _Mentees_ with learning
  and building their skill-set.

- **Expert**: Someone with enough experience to assisst _Peers_ on
  the spot without a lengthy onboarding process. _Experts_ can both, assist
  _Mentees_ in their learning, and, at the same time, show _Mentors_ to how
  to approach such a problem in the future without needing assistance by an
  _Expert_.

- **Peer-Group**: A _flat_ group -- without hierarchy -- of people on different
  skill levels, who agree to meet on a regular basis to a) learn themselves and
  b) assiss other group members in their learing process. Over time, temporary
  _Peer-Mentoring_ relationships may form, involving additional close
  collaboration among a subset of group members.

- **Peer-Mentoring**: A _One-on-One_-mentoring setup where a more experienced
  _Peer_ meets with a less experienced _Peer_ on a regular basis to learn how
  to apply the skill.  The more experienced _Peer_ assumes the role of a
  _teaching assisstant_ and may pass on their acquired experience while
  learning from the less experienced Peer's difficulties. For issues beyond
  their capacity, they may consult an _Expert_ for advice.

- **Expert-Mentoring**:  A _One-on-One_-mentoring setup where an _Expert_ meets
  with a _Peer_ on a regular basis to apply the skill to a real world problem.


> [!NOTE]
> Accessibility to one or more _Expert(s)_ is crucial for guidance.
> When a group starts out, it may be the only access to the field for its
> members. Once the group is well established, it may suffice to include the
> _Expert_ only occasionally or on demand, freeing capacity to support other --
> less mature -- peer groups.

> [!NOTE]
> A self-managed peer-mentoring group offers the opportunity to learn and
> practice organizing skills that are helpful when assuming a _Mentor_ role or
> engaging in a community, thus offering a personal development path.


## Structure

This playbook offers individual atomic pieces of advice that describe a a
problem and its likely consequences, and offers a tried approach to prevent or
solve the problem

- **[Template](Template.md)**: There will be a template covering relevant meta information
  about each bolierplate solution.

- **[Organizing](Organizing/README.md)**: Tips and skills helping to organize a peer group meeting on a
  regular basis.

- **[Properties](Properties/README.md)**: Tips regarding how to identify relevant properties for
  recurring meetings and how to define them.

- **[Peer-Mentoring](Peer-Mentoring/README.md)**: Tips and skills how to make peer mentoring a successful
  and enjoyable experience.

- **General Concpets**: Helpful concepts that may be helpful for a peer group
  as a learning community (e.g. Non-Violent Communication, Psychological
  Safety).
