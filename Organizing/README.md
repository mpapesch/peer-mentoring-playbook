# Organizing Skills

This acts as an index directory to the resources available to develop skills
for organizing peer-mentoring groups. For each skill, there should be a
fact-sheet describing its objective, intention and rationale; and/or
Observation, Effect, Need, Request.


- **[Shared Responsibility](shared-responsibility.md)**: Members of a
  peer-mentoring group own a shared responsibility for the existence of the
  group. Everyone needs to (learn to) step up and accept that responsibility.

- **Decision-Making**: A self-organized group needs a (democratic)
  decision-making process that is independent of a single individual.

- **Invites and Reminders**: Someone should step up and take -- temporary --
  responsibility through the Decision-Making Process to send out timely
  reminders for meetings, including the planned content.

- **Meeting in person at a conference**: While it sounds quite easy to 'just
  meet' at a conference, if there are more than five people involved and
  everyone is outside of their usual communication setting, the challenge of
  organizing a meeting at an unknown location can be reduced with little
  preparation.

- **Organize External Presenters**: When a peer-mentoring group is well
  established, it might be beneficial to invite external presenters every once
  in a while.

- **Invite _Experts_ for discussion**: When a peer-mentoring group is well
  established, it might be beneficial to invite an Expert to discuss a specific
  problem and establish personal connections every once in a while.

- **Collaborate with other Peer-Mentoring Groups**: A peer-mentoring group can
  be a point of entry into a field for people who are not part of the
  professional network yet. Building and maintaining such a network among
  peer-groups creates opportunities.
